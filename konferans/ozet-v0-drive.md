# Synthesis of Calcium Silicate Hydrate from a Waste-Derived Calcium Source

Barış BASMACI, Muge SARI YILMAZ

Department of Chemical Engineering, Yildiz Technical University, Davutpasa, Esenler, Istanbul, 34220

The cement industry accounts for approximately 8% of global CO₂ emissions, largely driven by clinker production. Reducing the clinker-to-cement ratio is a well-established decarbonization strategy; however, it typically results in a significant loss of early-age compressive strength. Calcium silicate hydrate (C-S-H) seeding admixtures have emerged as an effective solution to compensate for this strength loss by accelerating cement hydration kinetics and promoting early nucleation. Despite their proven efficacy, commercially available synthetic C-S-H admixtures rely on expensive and energy-intensive raw materials, limiting their widespread adoption. This study proposes a waste-valorization approach in which eggshell waste — a high-purity CaCO₃ source generated in excess of 200,000 tonnes annually in Türkiye — is converted into reactive CaO via controlled calcination at 950°C and subsequently used to synthesize C-S-H through hydrothermal reaction with sodium metasilicate pentahydrate. Synthesis was conducted under varying Ca/Si molar ratios (0.8–1.6), autoclave temperatures (140–180°C), and aging durations (24–72 h) to identify optimal production conditions. The resulting C-S-H powders were characterized by XRD-Rietveld quantitative phase analysis, FTIR spectroscopy, SEM-EDS, and TG/DTA. The most structurally suitable C-S-H was incorporated into CEM I 42.5N cement mortars at dosages of 1–5 wt.% alongside a polycarboxylate ether (PCE) dispersant. Mechanical performance was assessed at 2, 7, and 28 days according to TS EN 197-1, while hydration were monitored with XRD analysis. Phase evolution at each curing age was tracked by XRD-Rietveld and FTIR on solvent-arrested paste samples. This research addresses a critical gap in the literature, as no prior study has evaluated eggshell-derived C-S-H as a seeding admixture in cementitious systems. By converting a food industry by-product into a high-value construction material, the study contributes to both circular economy objectives and low-carbon cement technology. The findings are expected to provide a scientifically grounded, cost-effective, and locally sourced alternative to imported commercial C-S-H admixtures.

Keywords: Calcium silicate hydrate, Eggshell waste, Hydrothermal synthesis, Cement hydration.
